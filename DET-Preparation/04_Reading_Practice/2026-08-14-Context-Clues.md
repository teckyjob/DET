# Reading Practice — Context Clues & Word Class

## Date
2026-08-14

## Goal
Use context clues to infer meaning and identify word class from sentence structure.

## Practice 1 — reluctant

### Sentence
Although Maya was initially **reluctant** to speak during meetings, she gradually became more confident after her manager encouraged her to share her ideas.

### My Answers
- Meaning: **B. unwilling or hesitant**
- Context clue: `after her manager encouraged her to share her ideas`
- Word class: adjective

### Why
`reluctant` describes Maya's state after the linking verb `was`.

Pattern:
- `Maya was reluctant.`
- `S + be + adjective`

## Practice 2 — ambiguous

### Sentence
The instructions were so **ambiguous** that several students interpreted them in different ways.

### My Answers
- Meaning: **B. confusing or open to more than one meaning**
- Context clue: `students interpreted them in different ways`
- Original word-class answer: verb
- Correct word class: **adjective**

### Why
The sentence structure is:

`The instructions` + `were` + `ambiguous`

So `were` is the verb, while `ambiguous` describes the instructions.

Pattern:
- `The instructions were ambiguous.`
- `The question was difficult.`
- `The explanation was confusing.`
- `S + be + adjective`

## Practice 12 — confidence / confident

### Sentence
His **confidence** increased after he successfully completed the presentation.

### My Answers
- `confidence` = noun
- Structure: `his + noun`

### My Rewrite
`After sucessfully completing the presentation, he became more confident.`

### Correction
`sucessfully` → `successfully`

Useful contrast:
- his **confidence** → noun
- became more **confident** → adjective

## Practice 13 — confidently

### Sentence
She answered the interview questions **confidently** because she had prepared thoroughly.

### My Answers
- `confidently` = adverb
- It modifies `answered`

### My Rewrite
`She prepared thoroughly and make her more confident to answer the question.`

### Refined Version
`She prepared thoroughly, and this made her more confident when answering the questions.`

### Key Corrections
- `make` → `made`
- `confident to answer` → `confident when answering` / `confident about answering`

## Practice 14 — clearly / clear

### Sentence
The speaker explained the idea **clearly**, so everyone understood it immediately.

### My Answers
- `clearly` = adverb
- It modifies `explained`

### My Rewrite
`It is clear after the speaker explained the idea, so everyone understood it immediately.`

### Refined Version
`The speaker gave a clear explanation, so everyone understood the idea immediately.`

Useful contrast:
- a **clear** explanation → adjective + noun
- explained **clearly** → verb + adverb

## Current Assessment

### Strengths
- Context-clue inference is generally good.
- Can often infer unfamiliar vocabulary from consequences and surrounding meaning.
- `adjective + noun` is becoming stable.
- `verb + adverb` is becoming stable.
- `confidence` vs `confident` is improving.

### Improving
- `possessive + noun` was initially weak, but later answers with `curiosity` and `confidence` were correct.
- `S + be + adjective` is more stable than at the beginning of the session.

### Still Weak / Watch List
- Do not assume an unfamiliar word after `be` is a verb.
- Do not assume words after `his / her / my / your` are adjectives; they are often nouns.
- Maintain tense consistency when rewriting: `prepared` → `made`, not `make`.
- Spelling: `successfully`.

## Core Patterns

- `S + be + adjective`
  - Maya was reluctant.
  - The instructions were ambiguous.
  - The manager was skeptical.

- `possessive + noun`
  - her determination
  - his persistence
  - her curiosity
  - his confidence

- `adjective + noun`
  - a flexible schedule
  - a concise explanation
  - a substantial investment
  - a clear explanation

- `verb + adverb`
  - responded promptly
  - explained clearly
  - answered confidently

## Next Review Rule
When reviewing this topic, do not reveal the answer immediately. Give a new sentence with an unfamiliar or semi-familiar word and ask:

1. What does the word probably mean?
2. Which words in the sentence are the context clues?
3. Is it a noun, verb, adjective, or adverb? Why?

Mix noun, adjective, and adverb examples rather than drilling only one word class.
